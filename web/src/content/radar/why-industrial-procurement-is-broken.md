## The Economics of Lead Resale

If you run an engineering procurement department and submit an inquiry for an industrial transformer or CNC component on a mainstream Indian B2B portal, an automated sequence triggers:

Your technical inquiry is stripped into a generic lead label, matched to keyword tags, and immediately resold to 5 to 10 paying subscribers. Within twenty minutes, your phone rings repeatedly with calls from trading desks, brokers, and regional distributors. Frequently, none of them operate the production machinery or testing bays required for your exact specification.

For the portal, this model optimizes commercial monetization: a single buyer inquiry generates multiple monetization events. For technical manufacturing, it introduces noise and friction.

---

### Three Core Breakdowns in Legacy B2B Directories

#### 1. Ad Spend Trumps Technical Competence
On traditional marketplaces, search rank is governed by subscription tiers and sponsored ad spend. A trading company with no testing facilities can outrank an ISO-certified, CPRI-tested manufacturer simply by purchasing higher keyword priority.

#### 2. Technical Specifications Are Flattened
Industrial equipment cannot be evaluated like consumer goods. An engineer does not search for "electrical panel." They search for:
* *33kV AIS panel, 25kA for 3 sec, IP54 enclosure, IEC 62271 type-tested.*

When a directory lacks structured parameter fields for voltage classes, machine tonnages, testing standards, and certifications, technical capabilities are relegated to unstructured free-text descriptions.

#### 3. The Telemarketing Penalty on Suppliers
Manufacturers who list contact numbers openly on legacy portals face an influx of unsolicited sales pitches, irrelevant RFQs, and automated calls. Serious engineering teams eventually disengage, leaving profiles unmanaged.
